> Ethical monotheism is a form of exclusive monotheism in which God is believed to be the only god as well as the source for one's standards of morality, guiding humanity through ethical principles.

> **Ethical Theism** is a philosophical and religious position that combines a belief in God (or a higher power) with a strong emphasis on **morality and ethical conduct** as the central expression of religious life. Here's a summary of its key aspects:
> 
> ### Core Beliefs
> 
> - **Belief in God**: Affirms the existence of a supreme being or divine reality, though often conceived in a more rational or philosophical sense rather than strictly dogmatic.
> - **Morality as Central**: The primary focus of religion is **living ethically** — doing good, acting justly, and treating others with compassion.
> - **God as Moral Foundation**: God is seen primarily as the **source or grounding of moral law**, rather than a being defined mainly by ritual worship or supernatural intervention.
> 
> ### Key Characteristics
> 
> - De-emphasizes rigid **dogma, creeds, or ritual** in favor of ethical action
> - Draws heavily from **reason and philosophy** alongside faith
> - Often aligns with thinkers like **Immanuel Kant**, who grounded morality in rational duty
> - Values **human dignity, justice, and compassion** as expressions of divine will
> 
> ### How It Differs from Other Religions
> 
> | Aspect | Ethical Theism | Traditional Theism |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | Focus | Moral living | Doctrine & worship |
> | God's role | Moral lawgiver | Personal/interventionist deity |
> | Ritual | Minimized | Often central |
> | Scripture | Interpreted ethically | Often taken literally |
> 
> ### In Summary
> 
> Ethical Theism essentially argues that **the truest form of worship is moral action** — that believing in God means striving to live a good, just, and compassionate life above all else.
